### Question 1 — Answer: **C**

- **Why correct:** the broker log states the whole situation: `ISR Set(4)` against `min.isr requirement of 2`. The partition still has a **live leader** (which is also why `OfflinePartitionsCount` is 0) — the only thing missing is a second in-sync replica, and the only thing that brings one back is the two rack-B brokers. `NotEnoughReplicasException` is retriable, so producers keep retrying in the background until `delivery.timeout.ms` expires; restoring the rack is both the fastest and the only non-destructive path.
- **Why the others are wrong:** A — lowering `min.insync.replicas` mid-incident is the classic panic move. It restores writes by accepting them onto a **single** replica, so the very outage you are in becomes a data-loss window. B — `unclean.leader.election.enable` is about electing a leader when there is none; here there already is one, so it changes nothing except adding a standing data-loss risk cluster-wide. D — `num.replica.fetchers` speeds up followers that are *running*; the followers here are powered off.
- 🧠 **Key point / trap:** `OfflinePartitionsCount = 0` with `NotEnoughReplicasException` means *"reads fine, `acks=all` writes blocked"*. The fix is on the broker-availability side, never on the durability-setting side.
- 📎 Source: `../../study-plan/week-03/resources/kafka-design-replication.md` and `../../CCAAK-STUDY-PLAN.md` §7 (row *"Producer nhận `NotEnoughReplicas`"*).

### Question 2 — Answer: **B**

- **Why correct:** split `TotalTimeMs` into its phases before touching anything. Of the 1,840 ms, **1,610 ms is `RequestQueueTimeMs`** — time spent waiting for a request-handler (I/O) thread to pick the request up. `LocalTimeMs` (9 ms) and `RemoteTimeMs` (22 ms) show that the actual work is fast. `RequestHandlerAvgIdlePercent = 0.06` is far below the 0.3 floor, which is the documented signal for "the I/O pool is saturated". Raise `num.io.threads` above its default of 8.
- **Why the others are wrong:** A — `NetworkProcessorAvgIdlePercent = 0.71` says the network pool has plenty of headroom; raising `num.network.threads` (default 3) adds threads where there is no queue. C — `queued.max.requests` (default 500) is the depth of the very queue that is backing up; making it deeper lets more requests wait longer, it does not make them be served. D — `replica.lag.time.max.ms` governs ISR eviction, and 22 ms of `RemoteTimeMs` is a healthy `acks=all` follower wait, not a symptom.
- 🧠 **Key point / trap:** `RequestQueueTimeMs` high → not enough I/O threads. `LocalTimeMs` high → leader-side CPU or disk work. `RemoteTimeMs` high on Produce → waiting for followers. Read the phase, then choose the knob.
- 📎 Source: `../../study-plan/week-07/resources/kafka-monitoring-metrics.md` and `../../CCAAK-STUDY-PLAN.md` §6 (`num.io.threads` 8, `num.network.threads` 3, `queued.max.requests` 500).

### Question 3 — Answer: **A**

- **Why correct:** `No subject alternative names present` is hostname verification failing. `ssl.endpoint.identification.algorithm` has defaulted to `https` since **2.0**, and it compares the address the client actually dialled with the certificate's SAN entries. The working clients dial `broker-1.kafka.internal`, which is a DNS SAN; the new client dials a raw IP, and an IP only matches an `IP:` SAN entry. Pointing the new service at the DNS name already in the SAN list is the fix that requires no certificate reissue and no security weakening.
- **Why the others are wrong:** B — a missing intermediate raises `PKIX path building failed` (see Question 53), not a SAN error, and it would break every client, not just one. C — a missing client keystore under `ssl.client.auth=required` fails with a "Received fatal alert: bad_certificate"-class error, and the brokers were not changed. D — `HTTPS` is not a value of that property; the valid values are `https` (the default) and the empty string, and the property lives on the **client**.
- 🧠 **Key point / trap:** "works for everyone except the one client that connects by IP" is the signature of SAN/hostname verification. Disabling verification "just for this client" is the tempting and wrong fix.
- 📎 Source: `../../study-plan/week-05/resources/kafka-ssl-operations.md` and `../../study-plan/VALIDATION.md` (hostname verification on by default since 2.0).

### Question 4 — Answer: **D**

- **Why correct:** Connect restarts a failed **worker**'s work by rebalancing; it does **not** restart a failed **task**. A task that throws an unrecoverable exception stays `FAILED` until someone restarts it, and the connector object can be `RUNNING` the whole time — which is exactly why the third of the partitions assigned to task 1 has been untouched for 40 minutes with no alert. The targeted restart is `POST /connectors/{name}/restart?includeTasks=true&onlyFailed=true`. And it will fail again immediately unless the `ORA-00001` duplicate key is dealt with first (idempotent upsert, or a DLQ).
- **Why the others are wrong:** A — there is no automatic task restart, and both tasks 1 and 2 are on `10.0.3.15`, where task 2 is happily `RUNNING`, so the worker is not partitioned. B — a task with a recorded `trace` restarts in place perfectly well; deleting and re-creating the connector is the sledgehammer, and for a sink it also rejoins the same consumer group anyway. C — a bare `POST /restart` restarts only the **connector object**; `includeTasks=true` is what cascades to tasks. That default is the single most common Connect operational mistake.
- 🧠 **Key point / trap:** *connector `RUNNING` ≠ healthy*. Always read `tasks[].state`, and remember the two query parameters: `includeTasks` and `onlyFailed`.
- 📎 Source: `../../study-plan/week-06/resources/connect-rest-api-reference.md` and `../../CCAAK-STUDY-PLAN.md` §7 (row *"Connect task FAILED"*).

### Question 5 — Answer: **A**

- **Why correct:** `kafka-storage.sh random-uuid` mints a **brand-new** cluster id every time it is run. Every node of one cluster must be formatted with the **same** id, and the exception spells out the mismatch between what is in the new broker's `meta.properties` and what the quorum reports. The remedy is to read the real id (from a running node's `meta.properties`, or from `kafka-metadata-quorum.sh describe --status`, which prints `ClusterId`), wipe the new broker's log directories and re-format with it.
- **Why the others are wrong:** B — `--no-initial-controllers` is exactly right for a broker-only node joining an existing dynamic quorum; `--standalone` is for bootstrapping a **single-node** quorum and would be actively wrong here. C — `/brokers/ids/4` is a ZooKeeper znode. ZooKeeper was removed in 4.0; in KRaft a broker registers with the controller quorum over the controller listener. D — cluster ids are not derived from node ids, and a node-id collision produces a duplicate-id error, not this one.
- 🧠 **Key point / trap:** `random-uuid` is run **once per cluster**, at the very beginning — never once per node. Scripts that call it inside a per-node provisioning loop are the usual culprit.
- 📎 Source: `../../study-plan/week-01/resources/kafka-quickstart-and-storage-format.md` and `../../CCAAK-STUDY-PLAN.md` §6 (KRaft: *format với cùng `cluster.id`*).

### Question 6 — Answer: **C**

- **Why correct:** 1-Y · 2-Z · 3-W · 4-X. `NotEnoughReplicasException` on one topic only (1) is the leader refusing `acks=all` because that topic's ISR is under its `min.insync.replicas` (Y). `Topic not present in metadata` from a client that *did* reach the bootstrap server (2) is the `advertised.listeners` signature — bootstrap works, the returned addresses do not (Z). `OffsetOutOfRangeException` after a long outage (3) means retention deleted the segments holding the committed position (W). `CommitFailedException` in a group that rebalances every few minutes (4) is `max.poll.interval.ms` eviction: the member was removed before its commit landed (X).
- **Why the others are wrong:** A swaps 2 and 3, i.e. it blames retention for a metadata failure and a listener for an offset failure. B moves the ISR cause onto the metadata symptom and shifts everything by one. D pairs the poll-interval cause with the ISR symptom (`1-X`) and the ISR cause with the commit failure (`4-Y`), reversing the broker-side / client-side boundary entirely.
- 🧠 **Key point / trap:** each of these four exceptions has exactly one classic cause. Learn them as a set: ISR under min — listeners — retention — poll interval.
- 📎 Source: `../../study-plan/week-07/README.md` (bảng exception → nguyên nhân) and `../../CCAAK-STUDY-PLAN.md` §7.

### Question 7 — Answer: **A, D**

- **Why correct:** A — KIP-1066 (Kafka 4.3) lets you **cordon** a log directory so the controller stops placing new partitions on it. The broker keeps serving the 611 partitions already there, so this is instant, online and completely reversible; it stops the bleeding while you work. D — `kafka-reassign-partitions.sh` with a plan whose `log_dirs` array names the destination directory moves replicas **between directories on the same broker**, throttled with `--replica-alter-log-dirs-throttle`. That is the mechanism that actually rebalances 611 against 118.
- **Why the others are wrong:** B — deleting `.log` files under a running broker's data directory corrupts the partition: the index, time index and checkpoints no longer match, and the broker either refuses the directory or serves a broken log. It also requires the downtime the question forbids. C — `log.retention.bytes` is a **per-partition** cap, so with 611 partitions on `/data/d1` any value large enough to be safe is far too large to relieve the skew, and any value small enough to relieve it deletes data from every topic on every broker. It is irreversible and untargeted — the definition of "right knob, wrong problem". E — removing a directory from `log.dirs` and restarting means downtime, and it forces a full re-replication of 611 partitions from their leaders, which is the most expensive way to reach the same place.
- 🧠 **Key point / trap:** a full log directory has two cheap, reversible moves — **cordon** it, then **move partitions inside the broker**. Everything involving `rm` or a restart is a downgrade on both counts.
- 📎 Source: `../../study-plan/week-02/resources/kip-1066-cordoned-log-dirs.md` and `../../study-plan/week-02/resources/kafka-log-dirs-tool.md`.

### Question 8 — Answer: **B**

- **Why correct:** every pain point in the drill is something Cluster Linking removes. The destination brokers pull directly from the source, so there is no Connect fleet to run. Mirror topics keep the **source topic name** — no `prod.` prefix, so no application is looking at the wrong name. Offsets are **byte-for-byte identical**, so there is no offset translation and no replayed duplicates. Consumer offsets and ACLs are synced, so failover is a promote plus a client repoint. Both sides run Confluent Server, which is the prerequisite.
- **Why the others are wrong:** A — it does address the two symptoms, but it keeps the MM2 fleet, keeps offset translation as a *translation* (offsets are still not identical, only approximated by checkpoints), and `IdentityReplicationPolicy` is unsafe if the topology ever becomes bidirectional (see Question 31). It is the "improve the thing we have" answer to a question that asked for 15 minutes. C — a stretch cluster removes failover but changes the failure model entirely: every `acks=all` write now pays a cross-site round trip, and a partition of the link can take the quorum with it. That is a different architecture, not a DR-drill fix. D — MirrorMaker 1 was **removed in Kafka 4.0**; it is not an option on any current cluster.
- 🧠 **Key point / trap:** the deciding phrase is *"failover under 15 minutes at the same RPO"*. Offset **identity** (Cluster Linking) versus offset **translation** (MM2) is the whole difference.
- 📎 Source: `../../study-plan/week-04/resources/confluent-cluster-linking.md` and `../../study-plan/week-04/resources/kafka-geo-replication-mm2.md`.

### Question 9 — Answer: **C**

- **Why correct:** `records-lag-max` measures the distance from the consumer's position to the **log end** offset — how far behind you are. `records-lead-min` measures the distance from the position to the **log start** offset — how much runway you have before retention overwrites you. A lead approaching zero means the cleaner is about to delete records the group has never read; it is the only metric that warns of *data loss* rather than *delay*. Raising `retention.ms` on that topic is immediate, reversible and buys the time needed to fix throughput.
- **Why the others are wrong:** A — this inverts the metric. Catching up is `records-lag-max` falling; `records-lead-min` falling means retention is closing in from behind. B — an eviction shows up as rebalances and `CommitFailedException`; here every partition is assigned and no exception has been logged. D — a hot key produces *lag* on some partitions, not a shrinking *lead*, and adding partitions is one-way and re-maps keys (see Question 27).
- 🧠 **Key point / trap:** lag = how late you are; **lead = how much time you have left**. Alert on both. Only the second one predicts silent loss.
- 📎 Source: `../../study-plan/week-07/resources/confluent-consumer-lag-monitoring.md`.

### Question 10 — Answer: **A**

- **Why correct:** the exception names the resource type — **Group** — and reading a topic as part of a consumer group requires `Read` on the Group resource in addition to `Read`/`Describe` on the Topic. The console consumer succeeded precisely because `--partition 0` puts it into `assign()` mode: a standalone consumer joins no group, commits no offsets through a coordinator, and therefore needs no Group ACL at all. That contrast is the clue the question is built around.
- **Why the others are wrong:** B — the coordinator lookup (`FindCoordinator`) is not gated on a Cluster `Describe` ACL; if it were, the error would be a `ClusterAuthorizationException`. C — `__consumer_offsets` is managed by the broker; clients never need a topic ACL on it, and Group ACLs are not derived from it. D — `allow.everyone.if.no.acl.found` is a boolean broker property, not a list of groups, it defaults to `false`, and it only relaxes resources that have **no ACL at all**.
- 🧠 **Key point / trap:** "console consumer works, application does not" almost always means the application is doing something the console consumer was not — here, joining a group. Read the resource type in the exception name: `Topic…`, `Group…`, `Cluster…` each point at a different `--operation`/`--resource` pair.
- 📎 Source: `../../study-plan/week-05/resources/kafka-authorization-acls.md` and `../../CCAAK-STUDY-PLAN.md` §7 (row *"`GroupAuthorizationException`"*).

### Question 11 — Answer: **B**

- **Why correct:** the election ladder with ELR is **ISR → ELR → last known leader**. Here `Isr:` is empty and `Elr: 5`, and broker 5 is back and unfenced, so the controller elects it. This is a **clean** election: because the high watermark cannot advance while the ISR is smaller than `min.insync.replicas`, an ELR member is guaranteed to hold every committed record. No configuration change is needed and no data is lost — let it complete, then bring 2 and 8 back to rebuild the ISR.
- **Why the others are wrong:** A — the last known leader is the **last** resort, not the first, and waiting for broker 2 when a safe candidate is already available is a self-inflicted outage. Enabling unclean election here would trade a clean recovery for a lossy one. C — ELR members are elected automatically; `--election-type unclean` would deliberately allow a replica from *outside* ISR ∪ ELR, which is the opposite of what you want. D — `Elr:` lists **eligible** replicas, not excluded ones; `LastKnownElr: 2` is the separate record of who led last.
- 🧠 **Key point / trap:** ELR exists precisely so that "ISR is empty" no longer means "wait, or lose data". Learn the ladder, and remember a replica rebuilt on an empty disk drops out of the ELR.
- 📎 Source: `../../study-plan/week-03/resources/kafka-eligible-leader-replicas.md`.

### Question 12 — Answer: **D**

- **Why correct:** the order is 4 → 2 → 3 → 1. Step 4 is free and rules out the most common cause in one command — a group with no live members, or one stuck rebalancing, explains rising lag by itself. Step 2 is also free and **splits the problem space in half**: every partition behind points at the consumers or the brokers, one partition behind points at a hot key. Step 3 is the first step that costs a dashboard trip and it answers "is the cluster slow or is the application slow". Step 1 is the only step that **changes** anything, and raising the partition count is irreversible, so it goes last.
- **Why the others are wrong:** A (2 → 4 → 1 → 3) — puts the irreversible change third, before you have even looked at the brokers. B (4 → 3 → 2 → 1) — checks the brokers before the per-partition split, so you carry a dashboard investigation you may not need. C (3 → 4 → 2 → 1) — starts with the most expensive observation and only then asks whether the group has members at all.
- 🧠 **Key point / trap:** order diagnostic steps by **cost ascending, information descending**, and put every irreversible action last. Adding partitions is the single most common "fix" that makes the next incident worse.
- 📎 Source: `../../study-plan/week-07/README.md` (cây quyết định consumer lag) and `../../CCAAK-STUDY-PLAN.md` §7.

### Question 13 — Answer: **B, C**

- **Why correct:** B — `errors.tolerance=all` tells Connect to **discard** any record that fails conversion, transformation or delivery, and with `errors.log.enable=false` it does so without writing a line anywhere. No task fails, so no alert fires: six weeks of `RUNNING` with 0.4 % of records missing is the exact fingerprint of this combination. C — a DLQ captures the failing records, and `errors.deadletterqueue.context.headers.enable=true` adds the headers that say **why** each one failed (connector, task, topic, partition, offset, exception). Without the flag you keep the payload but lose the diagnosis.
- **Why the others are wrong:** A — `errors.tolerance=none` makes the **task fail** on the first bad record. That is loud and safe, but there is no automatic `__connect_errors` topic; the DLQ has to be configured by name. D — `errors.retry.timeout` governs retries of *retriable* delivery errors; these records fail conversion or the constraint check and are not retriable, and `-1` would simply retry forever. E — `total-records-skipped` is part of `task-error-metrics`, which is published for **sink and source** tasks alike; and a metric nobody alerts on would not have caught this either.
- 🧠 **Key point / trap:** `errors.tolerance=all` **without** a DLQ is silent data loss with a green dashboard. If you set it, set `errors.deadletterqueue.topic.name` in the same change — and DLQ is a **sink-only** feature.
- 📎 Source: `../../study-plan/week-06/resources/connect-error-handling-dlq-kip298.md` and `../../CCAAK-STUDY-PLAN.md` §6 (*DLQ chỉ sink connector*).

### Question 14 — Answer: **A**

- **Why correct:** `LeaderId: -1` with `HighWatermark: -1` means the Raft quorum has **no elected leader**. With 2 of 3 voters unreachable there is no majority, so nothing can be committed to the metadata log: no topic creation, no partition leader election, no broker registration, no ACL change. The **data plane** is a different story — each broker keeps serving from the metadata it has already applied, which is why the payments service never noticed. The remedy is to restore a second controller and regain the majority.
- **Why the others are wrong:** B — brokers are **observers** of the metadata log by design; they replicate it without voting. That is the normal, correct topology, not a misconfiguration. C — an election that cannot reach a majority never completes, however long you wait; raising `controller.quorum.election.timeout.ms` (default 1000 ms) changes nothing when two of three voters are gone. D — `controller.quorum.fetch.timeout.ms` (default 2000 ms) makes a voter **stand for election**; standing is not winning, and a single voter out of three can never win.
- 🧠 **Key point / trap:** *"control plane frozen, data plane still serving"* is the shape of a lost controller majority. If you can produce but cannot create a topic, check the quorum before you check anything else.
- 📎 Source: `../../study-plan/week-01/resources/kraft-operations-production.md`, `../../CCAAK-STUDY-PLAN.md` §6 (KRaft timers), and `../../study-plan/VALIDATION.md` (mục *"Mất đa số controller quorum thì data plane ra sao?"*).

### Question 15 — Answer: **B**

- **Why correct:** tiered storage has **two** retention settings per topic. `retention.ms` is the total across both tiers; `local.retention.ms` is how much stays on the broker's disk. Its default is **-2**, which means *"inherit the total"* — so with `retention.ms` set to 400 days and `local.retention.ms` untouched, the local tier is also keeping 400 days. Objects appear in the store (the copy path works) while the disk never drains (the local-delete path has nothing to do). Set `local.retention.ms` — or `local.retention.bytes` — to a few hours.
- **Why the others are wrong:** A — local deletion is not a function of segment count; a segment becomes locally deletable once it is copied **and** past `local.retention.*`. Waiting will not help because that threshold is 400 days away. C — the configuration clearly took effect, because objects are being written; and the topic name in the store would show otherwise. D — tiered storage works with `cleanup.policy=delete`; combining it with `compact` is unrelated and is not a precondition.
- 🧠 **Key point / trap:** `-2` means **"use the total retention"**, not "unlimited" and not "zero". Every tiered-storage rollout that forgets `local.retention.*` produces exactly this symptom: full object store, full disks.
- 📎 Source: `../../study-plan/week-02/resources/kafka-tiered-storage.md` and `../../CCAAK-STUDY-PLAN.md` §6 (*`log.local.retention.ms` mặc định -2*).

### Question 16 — Answer: **C**

- **Why correct:** this is the most dangerous upgrade trap in the 4.1 → 4.3 range. When ELR is enabled, `min.insync.replicas` **stops being a broker-level property** and must be set at the **cluster** level (`kafka-configs.sh --entity-type brokers --entity-default`) or per topic. Every topic that had been silently relying on each broker's `server.properties` value now falls back to the shipped default of **1** — which the `synonyms={DEFAULT_CONFIG:min.insync.replicas=1}` line in the output states outright: there is no broker-level synonym left to inherit from. Set it at cluster level, then re-audit every topic that matters.
- **Why the others are wrong:** A — `--describe --all` prints the effective value **plus** its provenance; that is precisely why it is the right tool here, and re-running without `--all` would hide the evidence. B — finalizing a feature flag does not touch topic configuration; there is nothing to restore. D — ELR improves *availability* when the ISR empties, by guaranteeing that an ELR member holds every committed record. It does not replace `min.insync.replicas`, which is what decides whether a write is accepted in the first place. With an effective value of 1, a single surviving replica can acknowledge `acks=all`, and ELR cannot invent durability that was never required.
- 🧠 **Key point / trap:** read the `synonyms={...}` list, not just the value. When the only synonym left is `DEFAULT_CONFIG`, something that used to supply the value has gone away.
- 📎 Source: `../../study-plan/week-03/resources/kafka-eligible-leader-replicas.md` and `../../study-plan/week-08/resources/kafka-4x-operational-changes.md` (ELR mặc định từ 4.1 và `min.insync.replicas` ở mức broker bị gỡ).

### Question 17 — Answer: **B**

- **Why correct:** the WARN line names the cause verbatim — the gap between `poll()` calls exceeded `max.poll.interval.ms`. With the default `max.poll.records=500` and 4–9 s per record, one batch needs hours, while the interval allows 300,000 ms. The member is evicted mid-batch, which triggers a rebalance, which the next member then also fails, which is why the state oscillates `PreparingRebalance → CompletingRebalance → PreparingRebalance`. 18 % CPU confirms the consumers are waiting on an external API, not saturated. Lowering `max.poll.records` (and/or raising the interval) is a client-side, instantly reversible change.
- **Why the others are wrong:** A — the group is not partition-starved; it is being evicted. Adding partitions is irreversible, re-maps keys, and would not stop the eviction because each member would still take 500 slow records per poll. C — under the classic protocol `session.timeout.ms` covers heartbeats, which a background thread keeps sending; under the KIP-848 protocol the broker owns liveness and the client value is ignored entirely. ### Question 23 — Answer: **D**

- **Why correct:** a three-voter Raft quorum needs **two** live voters to make progress. ### Question 24 — Answer: **A, C**

### Question 27 — Answer: **A**

Step 2 last: because `controller.quorum.auto.join.enable` defaults to **`false`** in 4.3, a started controller does **not** join by itself; `add-controller` is an explicit step, and `describe --status` is how you confirm three voters again.
- **Why the others are wrong:** A (1 → 3 → 4 → 2) — formats and starts the replacement before anyone has checked the quorum can accept a change. B (3 → 1 → 2 → 4) — adds the new voter while the dead one is still counted, which briefly makes a 4-voter quorum whose majority is 3, of which one is permanently unreachable: a fragile intermediate state. C (4 → 3 → 1 → 2) — changes the voter set first and verifies afterwards, which is the wrong order for any irreversible metadata operation.
- 🧠 **Key point / trap:** with `kraft.version: 1` (dynamic quorum) a controller swap is two explicit commands — **remove first, then add** — and in 4.3 nothing joins automatically. With `kraft.version: 0` (static) the voter set lives in `controller.quorum.voters` on every node and the swap needs a full restart instead.
- 📎 Source: `../../study-plan/week-01/resources/kraft-operations-production.md` and `../../study-plan/week-08/resources/kafka-4x-operational-changes.md` (`controller.quorum.auto.join.enable` = false ở 4.3).

### Question 55 — Answer: **C, D**

- **Why correct:** C — `unclean.leader.election.enable` is a **cluster-wide dynamic** broker config. The command takes effect with no restart, the controller immediately elects out-of-ISR replicas for partitions that have no leader, and `OfflinePartitionsCount` drops within seconds. That speed is exactly what makes it dangerous at 02:40. D — every partition that gains a leader this way truncates to the new leader's log end offset. Records that were committed and acknowledged to `acks=all` producers are gone; consumers that had read past that point see `OffsetOutOfRangeException` or silently start re-reading. Setting the flag back to `false` afterwards prevents the *next* unclean election and restores nothing.
- **Why the others are wrong:** A — the setting applies to existing partitions as soon as the controller evaluates them; there is no "new topics only" scoping. B — it is not read-only; the command output ("Completed updating default config for brokers in the cluster") confirms it was applied dynamically, which is precisely why the damage is already done. E — ELR makes unclean election **less often necessary**, because an ELR member can be elected cleanly when the ISR empties. It does not disable unclean election, and with ELR the right first move here is to check whether the partitions have an `Elr:` entry at all (Question 11).
- 🧠 **Key point / trap:** if you truly must accept the loss, `kafka-leader-election.sh --election-type unclean` on the **specific** partitions is far more contained than a cluster-wide default that outlives the incident. And note the audit trail: nobody wrote down which offsets were lost.
- 📎 Source: `../../study-plan/week-03/resources/kafka-design-replication.md` and `../../CCAAK-STUDY-PLAN.md` §6 (`unclean.leader.election.enable` **false**) · §7 (row *"`OfflinePartitionsCount` > 0"*).

### Question 56 — Answer: **B**

- **Why correct:** the workload is a **work queue**, and a consumer group is the wrong abstraction for it: one consumer per partition, offsets advancing in order, so a 30-minute record blocks everything behind it — which is exactly what the `--describe` output shows (partition 4 far behind, partition 7 current). Share groups (KIP-932, production-ready in **4.2**) give the queue semantics: many consumers may read the **same** partition, records are acknowledged individually and redelivered on failure. The costs are real and the question asks for them: no per-partition ordering, no offset-based rewind in the consumer-group sense, and a dependency on the internal topic `__share_group_state`, whose replication factor defaults to **3** — so a cluster with fewer than three brokers must lower `share.coordinator.state.topic.replication.factor` and `share.coordinator.state.topic.min.isr` to 1 first. `kafka-share-groups.sh` is the CLI.
- **Why the others are wrong:** A — 200 partitions makes the blocking finer-grained but does not remove it: one slow record still blocks its own partition, and the change is irreversible and re-maps keys. C — `max.poll.records=1` guarantees one record per poll and a long `max.poll.interval.ms` stops the eviction, so it is a genuine mitigation for the *rebalance* problem — but the partition is still processed strictly in order by one consumer, which is the actual complaint. D — KIP-848 changes how partitions are **assigned** (broker-driven, incremental); delivery semantics are unchanged, so head-of-line blocking is untouched.
- 🧠 **Key point / trap:** consumer group = ordered log consumption, parallelism bounded by partitions. Share group = queue, parallelism bounded by consumers, ordering surrendered. Pick by which guarantee the workload actually needs.
- 📎 Source: `../../study-plan/week-08/resources/kafka-4x-operational-changes.md` (share groups KIP-932, `__share_group_state` RF 3) and `../../study-plan/week-01/resources/basic-kafka-operations-admin-cli.md` (`kafka-share-groups.sh`).

### Question 57 — Answer: **D**

- **Why correct:** the broker's message is precise: the client offered `SCRAM-SHA-256`, and the listener's `sasl.enabled.mechanisms` contains only `SCRAM-SHA-512`. Two things follow. First, the client must set `sasl.mechanism=SCRAM-SHA-512`. Second — and this is the half people miss — a SCRAM credential is stored **per mechanism**: `kafka-configs.sh --describe` showing "a credential exists" does not mean a SHA-512 one exists. The credential must have been written with `--add-config 'SCRAM-SHA-512=[password=...]'`, otherwise the client will negotiate the mechanism successfully and then fail authentication.
- **Why the others are wrong:** A — adding SHA-256 back to every broker and rolling the cluster weakens the organisation's standard and costs a full cluster roll, all to accommodate one misconfigured client. It works, which is what makes it the tempting overreach. B — SCRAM credentials have not lived in ZooKeeper since 4.0; they live in the cluster metadata, and `--zookeeper` was removed from the CLI. C — this is an **authentication** failure whose text names the mechanism negotiation; an authorization problem would surface as `TopicAuthorizationException` or `GroupAuthorizationException` after a successful handshake.
- 🧠 **Key point / trap:** `SCRAM-SHA-256` and `SCRAM-SHA-512` are two separate credentials for the same user. Creating one and configuring the other is the most common SCRAM onboarding failure.
- 📎 Source: `../../study-plan/week-05/resources/kafka-sasl-operations.md` and `../../study-plan/VALIDATION.md` (CLI `--zookeeper` đã gỡ).

### Question 58 — Answer: **B**

- **Why correct:** a Kafka upgrade has two independent phases: the **binary** roll, and finalizing `metadata.version` with `kafka-features.sh upgrade`. While `metadata.version` is still at the old level the binary roll can be reversed — you can put 4.2 back on the brokers. Once it is finalized, whether a downgrade is possible depends entirely on whether the release introduced metadata changes, and **4.3.0 cannot be downgraded** (4.2.0 could; 4.0.1 and 4.1.0 could not). So the escape hatch should be kept open through the soak window and closed deliberately afterwards, verified with `kafka-features.sh describe`.
- **Why the others are wrong:** A — running on new binaries with the previous `metadata.version` is a supported, intended state; it is the whole reason the two phases exist. C — finalization has never been automatic; it is an explicit operator action precisely because it is the irreversible half. D — `downgrade` exists but is refused when the target metadata version cannot represent the current metadata, which is the case for 4.3.0. Assuming it always works is how a rollback plan becomes fiction.
- 🧠 **Key point / trap:** *"upgrade the bits, then soak, then finalize the metadata"*. The finalization is the point of no return, and whether it is reversible is a **per-release** fact you look up, not a general rule.
- 📎 Source: `../../study-plan/week-04/resources/kafka-rolling-upgrade-kraft.md`, `../../study-plan/week-07/resources/kafka-upgrade-notable-changes.md`, and `../../CCAAK-STUDY-PLAN.md` §6 (*Downgrade không phải lúc nào cũng được*).

### Question 59 — Answer: **A**

- **Why correct:** `describe --replication` lists voters and **observers** of `__cluster_metadata`. Node 5 is an observer — a broker — sitting 207,288 records behind, with `LastCaughtUpTimestamp` about four minutes older than the leader's clock. Brokers do not query the controller for every decision; they **apply** the metadata log locally and act on their own copy. So a broker that lags serves a stale world: topics it has not seen yet, ACLs it has not applied yet, leadership it still believes in. The topic is irrelevant — investigate that broker's disk latency on the directory holding the metadata log, and its network path to the controllers.
- **Why the others are wrong:** B — brokers are observers **by design**; promoting one to voter would put data-plane load in the consensus path and is not a remedy for slow replication. C — observers absolutely do replicate the metadata log; that is the whole architecture, and `Lag` is exactly the right column for them. `metadata.max.age.ms` governs a *client's* metadata refresh, a different layer. D — a follower three records behind is normal steady-state replication, and 2 of 3 voters (in fact all 3) are healthy with `LeaderId: 3001`; the quorum is fine.
- 🧠 **Key point / trap:** `describe --status` answers *"is there a quorum?"*; `describe --replication` answers *"is everyone keeping up?"*. For "this one broker behaves differently from the others", the second command is the one that finds it.
- 📎 Source: `../../study-plan/week-01/resources/kraft-operations-production.md` and `../../study-plan/week-07/resources/kafka-kraft-operations-debug.md`.

### Question 60 — Answer: **C**

- **Why correct:** a **sink** connector's offsets are the offsets of its consumer group, `connect-<connector-name>`, and `kafka-consumer-groups.sh --reset-offsets` refuses to touch a group that has live members — which the error states verbatim. `PUT /connectors/{n}/stop` puts the connector into `STOPPED`: the tasks are shut down and **deassigned**, so the group becomes empty and both rewind paths open up — Connect's own `PATCH /connectors/{n}/offsets` (KIP-875, `PATCH`/`DELETE` from **3.6**) or the CLI command already written. Then `PUT /connectors/{n}/resume`.
- **Why the others are wrong:** A — `PAUSED` stops processing but **keeps the tasks assigned**, so the consumer group still has members and the reset still fails. The `STOPPED` state was introduced precisely to provide the empty-group precondition. B — deleting and re-creating a sink connector with the same name gives it the same consumer group, so it resumes from the same committed offsets; nothing is rewound, and the three hours of work are re-done only if the offsets happen to be behind. D — it inverts the two cases: **source** offsets live in `connect-offsets`, **sink** offsets live in the consumer group. And `DELETE /connectors/{n}/offsets` resets to the beginning of the topic, not to 11:00, and it too requires `STOPPED`.
- 🧠 **Key point / trap:** `PAUSED` ≠ `STOPPED`. Any Connect offset operation needs `STOPPED`, and source and sink keep their offsets in completely different places.
- 📎 Source: `../../study-plan/week-06/resources/connect-offset-management-kip875.md` and `../../study-plan/VALIDATION.md` (KIP-875: `PATCH`/`DELETE /offsets` từ **3.6**, không phải 3.5).
